About

Commit Symphony turns a year of contributions into a 30-second song and lights the graph up as it plays. It is free, open source and made for GitHub users, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by GitHub.

How it works

  1. You type a username and a year. We read that person's public contribution graph.
  2. The graph becomes music: 52 weeks are 52 beats, the 7 weekdays are 7 notes of a scale, and how busy a day was sets how loud and layered its note is. Quiet weeks are never silent; a soft pad keeps playing.
  3. Your most-used language picks the style (lo-fi, synthwave, chiptune, house or ambient). Your username and year seed the key, mode, tempo and chord progression, so the same person always gets the same song.
  4. You can share a link, a picture card, the song as a WAV file, or a short vertical video.

Privacy

  • Only public contribution data is read, through GitHub's API, and only for the name you type.
  • No accounts, no sign-in, no cookies, no analytics, no ads. Your sign-in to GitHub is never involved.
  • Nothing about you is stored in a database. Results are cached for a few hours so the service stays fast and within GitHub's rate limits. Requests are counted per visitor for a minute to stop abuse, in memory only.
  • Pictures, songs and videos are made in your browser and never uploaded.

Made with

Preact, Tone.js for the audio, and a single serverless function for data. The source is MIT licensed: github.com/gyana-rj/magic-keyboard. The Inter typeface (SIL Open Font License) is used in link-preview images.
